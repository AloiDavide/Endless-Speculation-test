# Part 1: Beatrice, and the past.

Let's answer the greatest question first. What is Beatrice?
The answer comes in the form of one of the episode 4 tips.

> As a witch, Beatrice holds two titles: "Golden" and "Endless".
> These originally came from separate Magical Compendiums, so it can be said she wields the power of two compendiums.
> 
> The title of Endless Witch has its foundation in "Endless Creation" and is the root of her unmatched, endless magical power.
> The title of Golden Witch has its foundation in "the Realization of Magic", a magical power that can materialize imaginary precious metals, providing the miracle of manifestation to all less tangible forms of magic.
> 
> These two titles were refined even further with Mariage Sorcière leading to their sublimation into the compendium known as "Endless Realization".
> 
> In that sense, she technically shouldn't be referred as either the Endless Witch or the Golden Witch, but by a new title that is a fusion of the two.
> 
>~ [[Tips/Beatrice's Titles\|Beatrice's Titles]]

Because magic in Umineko is the act of holding and perpetrating beliefs, saying that Beatrice holds these titles is saying that she's the "head" of both the golden magic, and endless magic narratives at the same time. 
But, as Bernkastel pointed out, she is not "one individual woman", and the figureheads of the two compendiums haven't always been the same over the years.

## The golden witches

### Alpha Beatrice

At some point after the second world war, some lady gave Kinzo access to a vast amount of gold, and Kinzo fell desperately in love with her. Given his knack for the occult, he would then frame her as an all powerful witch.

We'll call this woman Alpha Beatrice. 
We went back and forth trying to decide if we should call her the first golden witch or exclude her by technicality. She sealed the original deal with Kinzo and would have had the rights to his fortune, but unlike her successors she isn't a witch of Kinzo's creation. 
Either way, she served as the inspiration for the branch of the golden magic.

Do the 10 tons of gold really exist? Banquet of the golden witch makes it really difficult to dismiss it as nothing but pure misdirection. Both Eva and Rosa get to see it in a scene that isn't overtly magical.
That being said, it's very conceivable that what Alpha gave Kinzo was just a starting capital to enter the world of business, and Kinzo amassed the rest of the wealth over the years. 

Even if the gold does exist, that amount is not easy to move, sell, or utilize. What this funding really did for Kinzo was give him chips to gamble with. He used the rumors around the gold as a buy in to access the business world and succeed in it, until he really became a multi billionaire and restored the name Ushiromiya to the importance it had lost after the war.

Truly, "a magical power that can materialize imaginary precious metals".

Alpha Beatrice eventually died some time before 1952 under circumstances as of yet unknown. 
![Pasted image 20260808111023.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260808111023.png)

Kinzo was distraught by this fact, and he became deeply obsessed with the myth of the witch he had created, so much so that eventually, he came up with an idea to "resurrect" her.

### 0 Beatrice

The Ushiromiya head eventually bought Rokkenjima, and on it he built the main mansion, as well as a secondary hidden one called Kuwadorian. 
In the secret mansion he would raise a secluded child, referring to her as Beatrice, treating her as his own possession and shielding her from any knowledge of the outside world. 
The only people who know about her situation are the higher ranking servants and Nanjo.

This is the Beatrice we see in Rosa's flashback who died by falling off a cliff in 1968. We'll call her 0 Beatrice, since we don't know her real name or if she ever had one.
She is the "first" golden witch in the sense that she was created by Kinzo's golden magic. Aka. he used his money to will a new witch Beatrice into existence after the previous one had died, just to fulfill his wish of seeing his love reciprocated. After all it's implied that his feeling for Alpha were one sided to the very end.

### X Beatrice

With Kinzo mourning his beloved for the second time, the supply trips to Kuwadorian stopped completely.

![Pasted image 20251215223913.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020251215223913.png)

Nobody was living there anymore, and in Beato's magical reconstruction the witch's soul now without a vessel was reduces to golden butterflies permeating the air of Rokkenjima.
It would seem for all intents and purposes like he had given up on the project, but this wasn't the end.

Kinzo eventually came across another child with unusual western traits, blue eyes, and golden blonde hair just like his beloved witch.  We're not sure where she came from but she might have been an orphan at Fukuin house, the orphanage he founded where the younger servants are raised.

This girl has been our mysterious 19th person X since episode two, and since she doesn't have a name either, we deem it fitting to call her X Beatrice. 
![X Beatrice.png](/img/user/Mugshots/X%20Beatrice.png)


In Turn of the Golden Witch she is the person that Rosa sees with Maria, and that Kyrie says she talked to (she does appear in more scenes, but those are the only reliable ones for reasons we will not reiterate now).
Here in Alliance she makes another appearance as is the person who talks to Battler from the balcony at the entrance of the mansion.

We think the exists because the way she is described by Kyrie and shown with Maria and Battler visually distinguishes her from the usual Beatrice. We'll later go to greater lengths to justify her, doing so now would be premature.

X is very likely the model for the witch's portrait that was hung in the hallway with the epitaph six years ago. Multiple people comment on her looking exactly like the painting, and although Rosa said the same of the "witch of the forest" she met all those years ago, it's reasonable to assume she only remembers her most striking features like the eyes and hair. Most likely Rosa's brain just filled in the details in her memory when she saw the painting after being conditioned to expect Beatrice.

Although we're calling her X Beatrice she doesn't become Beatrice right away, because of course Kinzo has learned a lesson from the experiment with 0 and he understands that raising a secluded child like that is risky and unsustainable.
The family is suspicious, and rumors are spreading about someone else living on the island, if you then consider that she was probably older than 0 Beatrice was at the time of her confinement, it just makes more sense to raise her in the orphanage. 
It's still a controlled environment but far away from Rokkenjima, and she can still periodically visit Kuwadorian through the hidden harbor.

Perhaps because of this less sheltered upbringing, we think X comes to strongly resent Kinzo's expectations. In his eyes she'll eventually fulfill the role of Beatrice like her predecessor, but the resurrection won't be complete until that happens.

During one of the the family conferences in 1980 somehow Battler accidentally meets X, and in that instance he makes some kind of promise to her, a promise without any real weight which he will forget in the six years that he'll spend away from the family, and this fact will break her heart.
### Kinzo's Furniture

Beyond Kinzo's Beatrice's project, another aspect of golden magic can be found in the servants that Kinzo calls his furniture. After all they're his property, an extension of his wealth, so he can forge them new identities.

The kids from the orphanage are given names that override their own when they become servants, of which Shannon and Kanon are two* examples.
Likewise, although we know almost nothing about Genji's relationship with Kinzo in the past, we can see he's fully devoted to his role as furniture and has been shaped by it.

### Golden magic

> The title of Golden Witch has its foundation in "the Realization of Magic", a magical power that can materialize imaginary precious metals, providing the miracle of manifestation to all less tangible forms of magic.

Overall, we can identify the golden magic branch as Kinzo and Beatrice's absolute authority over all things that belong to the Ushiromiya family head. This power lets them turn false objects or even people into reality by simply flaunting wealth and status, be them real of perceived.

The subjects include the gold, beatrice herself, the servants, as well as the mansion and the whole island. Likely even Kinzo's heirs (see the [[Tips/Beatrice's Letters/Letter E1-01\|first of Beatrice's letters in legend]]). More or less everything and everyone that was on the island at the time of the tragedy.

As more proof that the golden magic is strongly associated with the headship of the family, we know that the servants are taught to treat Lady Beatrice as the second master of Rokkenjima who must be treated with the same respect as Kinzo, and in the meta layer of the story, we see the title be inherited by Eva and eventually Ange when they become the successor.

## The endless witches

We have half of the puzzle figured out, now we can move on to the more elusive "endless" branch of endless realization.

We can start to define what this branch is by pointing out what is is not.
The adjective endless, as far as we're aware, is never mentioned by Kinzo or any other distinctly human character next to Beatrice's name, it instead features prominently in the tea parties as her main title since as soon as Bernkastel's first appearence at the end of Legend.
![Pasted image 20260830181903.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260830181903.png)

The reason for this is quite simple, endless magic has nothing to do with Kinzo's legend of the witch, but it originates from the coping mechanisms of the servants working under him.

### Alpha Beatrice

Why is she here? Didn't she kickstart the golden magic branch? 

Well yes, but that was Kinzo's side of the story. Now try putting yourselves in her shoes instead, having to deal with Kinzo's unrequited love, and having to watch him mistreat his servants, his children, and really anybody he considers his inferior. 
We don't know if Alpha herself was a servant, but we hypothesise that to them she was either an empathising companion or a caring master, perhaps both at different times, and the endless magic is her attempt to defend them from the harshness of the Ushiromiya head.

This claim is admittedly not based on much, because there isn't much to base any claims on. All we get in terms of a backstory for the endless branch is Virgilia's flashback in the prologue of Banquet, and in that she mentions that she herself had a master who taught her magic.

![Pasted image 20260830184418.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260830184418.png)

This master could be anybody, it doesn't have to be Alpha, but in trying to fill in the gaps we will go with the option that is the most thematically fulfilling, while also minimizing the number of unknown character we need to introduce.

So Alpha Beatrice is an endless witch. Not necessarily the first endless witch, as the tip Regarding the Succession of Witches hints at a long lineage, but she is the first we care about. 

> Furthermore, during the story, Beatrice inherited that name along with the position, but that is a very recent innovation. The Predecessor was the original Beatrice, and all Endless Witches before her had different names. After that, it became customary for the name to be inherited as well, and EVA Beatrice followed that tradition when giving ANGE the name of Beatrice.

This paragraph is very confusing because it seemingly introduces a contradiction. The predecessor it mentions is almost certainly Virgilia, and it's impossible for Alpha to be Virgilia because she died too early to interact with the young girl in the flashback, whoever she may be out of all possible candidates (and also because we know who Virgilia is already). 
This leaves us with the most plausible explanation being that Alpha never called herself Beatrice at all, and it's a name given to her by Kinzo. This is not too far fetched, as it's exactly the same argument we'll use for X Beatrice when the red truth requires Battler to have known her under a different name.

Either that or Virgilia's teacher is unrelated to Alpha after all.

### Virgilia
The equivalence Virgilia = Kumasawa is almost as spelled out in the text as Ronove = Genji. If her bursting out of Kumasawa in Banquet isn't enough evidence, shall we point at the mackerel joke?
![Pasted image 20250413161435.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020250413161435.png)


Kumasawa is the oldest servant of Kinzo, and would have been a young maid when Alpha Beatrice was still alive. It's through this connection that she was taught the ropes of her magic.

Now that she is older, she acts as a motherly figure towards the younger servants, many of whom are orphaned children from Fukuin house. She playfully calls herself beatrice in front of them, teaches them the ropes of the job, and as her master once did for her she also teaches them how to deal with the colorful and not always agreeable characters of the Ushiromiya family.

Let's zoom in on a certain clumsy servant, who is predictably bad at her job, and frequently damages and breaks things, as any child would. This servant is Sayo, the real name of the furniture named Shannon.

The flashback shows us an example of Kumasawa teaching Sayo the techniques of the endless magic, a way to make your narrative a reality regardless of what originally happened. 
After Sayo breaks a vase, Kumasawa "fixes it with magic". In truth we're made to understand that she talked to the other servants and convinced them to testify that the vase was broken by a cat.

![Pasted image 20260830190710.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260830190710.png)

This is pretty much all there is to it. Endless magic at its heart is telling white lies and cooperating to uphold them. It's a shared narrative that people believe together to cope with reality, and if the magic is internalized as real it can in turn influence the world.

This approach can be applied to more than just a trick to survive on the job, it can also be a form of escapism to help people cope with the reality of their situation.

### Mariage Sorciére

## The downward spiral of the Black witch

As the years pass and the children grow up, things don't improve for any of them. Escapism is not really a long term solution to living in an unhealthy environment, and for all three of our girls there must me a breaking point somewhere.

Starting with Maria, we know all the details of her struggles from the many scenes in alliance of Ange reading her diary. She's suffering because she feels like a burden for her mother, who's under immense amounts of financial and societal pressure and is starting to become abusive. 
In order to cope with this Maria applies the magic she learned from Sayo.

"Black witch" or black magic is a catch all term for people who act violent or abusive in order to cope with one's situation. The name is introduced as a way for Maria to rationalize the bad part of Rosa's personality, but later on the same framework is adopted by Ange, who applies it to Eva, Kasumi, and Beatrice (thus Sayo) herself.

Speaking of Ange, during the 1985 family conference, the last one before the tragedy, Maria tries to teach her about their magic and briefly admits her into the alliance... but,as you might expect from a 5 year old, Ange doesn't take it very seriously. She only sees it as a game and so she makes fun of Maria's fantasies, and in an act reminiscent of her brother she denies magic. 

> Young Ange did receive an invitation to join this alliance at one point, but she was later excommunicated.
> ~ Mariage Sorcière

An insinuation of her happiness being just a lie is the last drop for Maria, who starts wishing pain, not happiness, upon others. This is perfectly exemplified by the Chiester sisters characters, originally based on Maria's rabbits figurines that Rosa had gifted her, and now corrupted to represent guns in the new Mariage Sorciére.

In these three tips, the author goes out of his way to classify witches into three tiers: Creators, Voyagers, and a baseline lowest category that we will call Grounded witches. These categories are an important foundation for any would-be comprehensive theory of Umineko so let's spend a few more words on them.

### Grounded Witches

Grounded witches are people who have managed to exert influence over their own environment through "magic"... that is, through some form of shared belief. 
For example, we previously described how the golden magic manifested from Kinzo's obsession and the endless magic from the servants living under it and being forced to adopt coping mechanisms.
Because of this, for the longest time Beatrice existed on Rokkenjima as a grounded witch in those two forms, influencing the events of that fragment.

But hold up, what exactly is a fragment?
Well, the tips define them as "worlds of different fates and circumstances", and the one single fragment where a grounded witch can "use their power freely" is defined as their territory, but when the higher ranked witches talk about them they imply that there is a fragment for any possible timeline, akin to the concept of a multiverse in time travel stories, or the many worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics (alas, Ryuukishi is no stranger to including tropes that originate from quantum physics in his stories, no matter how tired they become).

![Pasted image 20260814124334.png|700](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260814124334.png)

There's one elegant way to interpret them that allows for both readings to be true. Fragments are the same as the rest of magic in Umineko, they're stories. Just one layer of abstraction higher.
They are made of a setting (different circumstances) and a scenario (different fates). The setting dictates what the initial state of the world is, who and what is relevant to the story, and what is physically possible; on the other hand the scenario dictates which of all the possible paths the story will take. If the author were to write about a different possibility, or even just imagine it, then that would become yet another unit in the sea of fragments.

Grounded witches are the weakest form of witch because their influence is limited in scope to just one fragment. They may perpetuate grand narratives, but they are still characters in their own story, unable to experience anything outside of their reality.

### Voyagers

Voyagers are witches who do not limit themselves to a single story. Instead, they enjoy exploring multiple fragments and spend their time interacting with them. They are readers who engage with various works and form opinions about them, but are disinterested in creation. What they seek is entertainment to stave off the poison of boredom. 

This means they can be as invested in (or detached from) the joys and sufferings of the characters as they please. After all, those characters ARE works of fiction. Hence the line about their values being ever-changing.

In the first half of _Umineko_, we encounter two Voyager witches: Bernkastel and Lambdadelta. As far as the story is concerned, we think it's appropriate to understand them as personifications of abstract concepts.
- Bernkastel embodies *miracles*: the ability to achieve success in spite of overwhelming odds.
- Lambdadelta embodies *certainty*: the ability to achieve one’s goals through overwhelming effort.

The two of them are not, nor do they represent, active participants in any of the events we've seen so far. Neither before, during, or after the murders in Rokkenjima. Although, from knowing of the existence of a certain character we suspect one of them might soon come up with a little self insert.


### Creators
What happens when you know not just everything that is, but everything that could be? That could get very boring indeed. That's why witches of the highest order are dubbed Creators.

We believe that what Creators create are new settings with their own rules and new scenarios within them. In other words they're the writers who construct vast, sprawling narratives that become worlds of their own.

Maria is likened to a creator because she helped bring Mariage Sorcière into existence by injecting the preexisting narratives with the creative spark that gave birth to all the colorful characters populating the tea party.

<b><font color="#00b0f0">Rule X is the magic of certainty.</font></b> In other words it's a starting condition where all paths lead to the same conclusion.

But as we know, this rule alone is not quite airtight. It was broken once and it can be broken again. Arguably it HAS been broken again judging from Eva's survival.
To make a truly perfect gameboard you need an element of deception that makes any flaws in the plan impossible to find.

### Rule Y
Bernkastel describes rule Y as the very existence of the witch Beatrice and the source of her magic. Her own original compendium.

![Pasted image 20260830105615.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260830105615.png)
 
Beato's power is compared to the letter's recipient because she "opened up a world" that the voyager witches didn't know about. This refers to how, through the bottled letters, Rokkenjima became a whole new setting contained inside the world of Umineko, but nowhere to be found in the sea of fragments that Bern has spent an eternity wandering.

Inside this pocket universe, shared worldviews are portrayed as true by the narrative.
This is why magic is so often shown on screen during the games, as long as nobody denies it the narration of the witch takes precedence. This is also why we get scenes of Kinzo monologuing to himself, or if he talks to other people, it's only those who know that he's already dead. Same for Shannon and Kanon who can interact with each other on screen, both alone and in the presence of the other servants.

<b><font color="#00b0f0">Rule Y is the endless magic.</font></b> The form of magic closest to the current acting Beatrice, Sayo.

But if this were all, you could experience enough versions of the story to eventually, painstakingly, converge on a solution. It would take Bern a long time, but not forever.
### Rule Z
This rule is the source of Beato's lethally bad affinity against Bernkastel, it's what makes sure that she can't eventually spend enough games fully understanding the events and reaching the truth.

![Pasted image 20260830121347.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260830121347.png)

==leggermente espansa con alcune letture su cui però non sono super sicuro==

If the previous rule was able to pretend that something was real, this can actually alter the identity of people and objects in Rokkenjima to fit the narrative.
Think of how during the murder scenes from episode two onward dead people are considered pieces taken by the witch and are therefore portrayed saying and doing things that shouldn't really be possible, and how with each episode the amount of magic happening on screen increases exponentially.

You can also see this rule in action when by wielding the red truth the witch is able to force certain notions such as "This door is impossible to open without a key". 
It doesn't lie, but it forces an interpretation of the facts, and it means that sometimes the wording used in the red text can be used to lead astray, like counting different identities as separate people or including Kinzo in the murder cases.
If you're able to mask uncertain information as certain and vice versa, you have the perfect weapon to subjugate the witch of miracles.

<b><font color="#00b0f0">Rule Z is the golden magic.</font></b> The power that Kinzo's legend of the witch holds over all theories and explanations in Rokkenjima. 
Like it does in Endless Realization, it serves to strengthen rule Y and turn the altered reality based on mutual consent into a forceful one that refuses to be denied.

It's interesting that after comparing Beato's power to her friend's, who's implied to be a Creator, and doubting if Voyagers should even be allowed to call themselves witches, Bernkaster says this:

> Unless I can unravel this rule Z... no, basically it's exactly because I can't unravel it that Beatrice is a witch.

At this point we can say with enough credibility that Beatrice is a kind of Creator witch. She is definitely capable of creating new fragments, but she sort of cheated her way to it by limiting her scope to a tiny slice of space and time, and she did it as a human in the world of Umineko. 
For this reason even though both of them recognize she did something incredible she's still treated like a being of an inferior order compared to the voyager witches.

## The Tea Party

Sayo writes the games to follow the rules of her magic to the letter. By doing so, she makes the narrative of the Golden and Endless Witch that emerges from them unshakable.

Captain Kawabata does his part. A fisherman recovers the first bottle containing “Legend of the Golden Witch,” and the police later take possession of it. Its contents are published, and the witch’s narrative sets the world ablaze.

From now on, we’ll call the fragments influenced by these letters the “future” fragments.

The legend attracts amateurs interested in solving its mysteries, witch hunters drawn to its magical narratives, and gossip seekers eager to learn about the scandalous events on Rokkenjima.

With endless magic as the vehicle and golden magic as the fuel, this intense interest in Rokkenjima takes shape as the tea party: a limbo where people debate the events of the tragedy. The tea party parallels the game in the higher world of witches, but Beatrice’s opponent here isn’t Bernkastel. It’s Battler Ushiromiya, the ideal rival for the witch, and the person who was supposed to stop the tragedy by remembering his forgotten promise.

The main takeaway is that the tea party is not a time loop endlessly repeating those two days. It takes place in hindsight, looking back on a mystery whose outcome has already been decided, even though the path to that outcome remains obscured. So when we follow Ange in Alliance, we don’t travel to the future. We simply stop looking back for a while and see the present.

The tea party is populated through Endless Realization (see the Mariage Sorciére chapter). This explains how Battler can be there even though he’s already dead: he is a character in this narrative, distinct both from the real life Battler and from the Battler portrayed in the letters. The same applies to Beato. Although she can largely be identified with Sayo, she also incorporates elements of all the Beatrices described in Part 1.

To understand the structure of this world and give it a proper name, we need to examine the concepts of Hell, Purgatory, and Paradise introduced throughout the question arcs.

### Hell

In _Umineko_, as in classical Western tradition, Hell is a place of torment where the dead are forced to suffer over and over.

It represents the failure state of the games described in the bottled letters: in Beatrice’s words, “everybody died and went to the golden land.” She presents this outcome as the only happiness left to them, as suggested from this sentence in the last of Battler’s profiles at the end of Alliance.

![Pasted image 20260802221757.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260802221757.png)

Hell could also plausibly be read as the gameboard itself, where we explore all the ways these people might have died.

### Purgatory

If Purgatory is the place between Hell and Paradise where souls endure trials before reaching the latter, then Umineko’s Purgatory is the tea party itself. It's not a surprise then, that it is the name given to the setting of the tea party at the end of Legend.

![Pasted image 20260822110232.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260822110232.png)

For this reason, we’ll refer to the tea party where Battler and Beatrice debate as “Purgatory,” just as we’ve been using “the higher world of witches” to refer to the plane of the Voyagers.

### Paradise

Umineko keeps returning to one question: what is Beatrice’s actual win condition? What is her Paradise?

We’re told repeatedly that she wants Battler to believe in her magic, but that goal can be interpreted in many ways. It’s a dilemma that troubles Purgatory Beato as well.

Her goal can’t simply be to make Battler admit defeat. He already did that in Turn, and the games continued. Nor can it be to make him accept a false reality where everyone is happy in the Golden Land and there’s no conflict left to resolve. That almost happened at the end of Banquet. But just before Ange arrived, Beatrice seemed like she was about to end the farce herself, likely because she realized it wasn’t the answer.

![Pasted image 20261003132319.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020261003132319.png)

Sayo’s deepest wish is something else. When she wrote the letters, the Paradise she and X hoped for may have been the genuine happiness that the people of Rokkenjima never reached: a world where Battler remembers his promise and the tragedy never happens.

That wish can no longer be fulfilled. Beato’s “Paradise” has instead become a world where the pain of Rokkenjima is understood and acknowledged, and where those who suffered finally receive some measure of true understanding.

The games Beato plays against Battler are difficult, but they aren’t unwinnable. She seems to be leading him toward an understanding of what happened on Rokkenjima, allowing him to reach it on his own. Lambdadelta, by contrast, might construct something so opaque that it couldn’t be solved using the available information. Many of Beato’s red truths become crucial hints. She repeatedly accepts losses she could have avoided, and, through Virgilia, she even helps explain how endless magic works.

Battler is Beato’s opponent because his failure to remember his sin embodies the lack of understanding she wants to overcome. His personality also makes him the perfect skeptic, though we’re sure Bernkastel cries herself to sleep knowing she got stuck with the incompetent one when she could have had Kyrie instead.

This also helps explain why Beato can’t simply tell him the truth. Sayo wrote the letters from a place of hopelessness, and the story deliberately emphasizes that Battler must understand his sin for himself.

We like to imagine this condition mirrored in the higher world of witches, with Lambdadelta imposing a rule on Beatrice as insurance. Like Ange, Beatrice cannot reveal her true name to Battler. He has to figure it out on his own.

==Da qua in poi potrebbe anche essere messo alla fine della parte 5==
Despite her creator-adjacent powers inside her cage, Beatrice is still a temporary witch. She needs Lambda’s approval, which means maintaining the strength of will that fuels the power of certainty. By Alliance of the Golden Witch, that will has been badly shaken.

At the end of the episode, Beato despairs at the apparent impossibility of her task. She no longer wants to fight or keep subjecting people to the “hell” of her Golden Land. She seems willing to be forgotten and die without reaching her Paradise but Battler gives her a reason to keep going.

It’s no coincidence that the question she leaves him with is “Who am I?”

![Pasted image 20261003144135.png](/img/user/Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020261003144135.png)

Here, Beatrice finally acknowledges what she wants. If Battler can solve the riddle, if he can understand and accept her, he will be invited to the “first true tea party.” ==citation needed==
## The tea party of those who are not humans

We've been writing around it this whole time, but why exactly do witches exist? Why does the higher level of magic exist? Much like the tea party is a place in which people discuss the lettered Bottles, The ??? Tea party is a place in which the characters of the visual novel discuss it's content. We've been calling it, up to now, the real world of witches.

So the tea party is a place where the characters of Rokkenjima discuss the contents of the lettered Bottles, and the ??? tea party, (or the higher world of witches, as we've been calling it) is a place in which the characters of the visual novel discuss its content. 
Can we go any higher?

Remember the recipient of Bernkastel's letter? Her and Lambdadelta's mutual friend? 
We believe they are not just any creator, but the creator of "Umineko no naku koro ni". After all this is a game written by someone, and the mechanics of it are established to work like a magical compendium in a consistent way.

# Part 4: The first three games

The reason we may have glossed over many details so far is that, as you might know or not, this is actually our fourth and last Umineko theory.

Previously, we combed through the first three episodes, painstakingly analyzing the games presented and trying to wrap our heads around the metaphysics. We wanted the final chapter to stand on its own, which is why we've already spent a word count equivalent to the longest of the three laying out everything we believe we know about Umineko in general.

In this part, we'll briefly sum up the main takeaways from the previous theories, but first we need to establish an important distinction between "games" and "episodes".

The "games" are Sayo's original bottled letters. Each is a story that begins with the setup of the gameboard and aims to convince the reader of the magical version of events while being based on a mundane one. By the time we experience them, the games are static. They were written twelve years ago and cannot change to challenge Battler.

The "episodes" are duels between Battler and Beatrice in the tea party, built around the "games". Of course, they're also episodes of the visual novel Umineko When They Cry, authored by its creator. Importantly, Beato can perform her magic over them, so certain things that weren't part of the original games may be shown to Battler, or to whoever is spectating.

We're not sure this distinction is always consistent in Umineko, but we need to make it to illustrate some elements of the games more clearly.

## Legend of the Golden Witch

This is our original theory for Legend of the Golden Witch.

### [[Long Form Theories/Theory for Legend of the Golden witch\|Theory for Legend of the Golden witch]]

_~ published on 18/04/2025_

We'll link it here for anyone interested, although reading it back makes us cringe somewhat. Being the first one, it's riddled with mistakes, and the writing isn't the best. The following reconstruction covers mostly the same arguments, but in better form and with some corrections, so nobody has to go through it.

Ironically, given that we've just distinguished between games and episodes, Legend of the Golden Witch is the one episode that coincides exactly with the game it depicts. This is because, before the first bottle surfaced, the conversation surrounding Rokkenjima was strictly materialistic. People treated the unsolved case as a murder mystery and wouldn't assume from the outset that magic was involved.

So, let's present our reconstruction.

- There are 17 people on Rokkenjima: Krauss, Natsuhi, Eva, Hideyoshi, Rudolph, Kyrie, Rosa, Jessica, George, Maria, Battler, Genji, Kumasawa, Gohda, Nanjo, Sayo and X.
- During the day, Sayo contacts Eva, Hideyoshi and Rudolph to invite them to participate in the plan. Rudolph refuses and is forced to stay silent under threat. Eva and Hideyoshi accept, and Sayo lets them in on the fact that Kinzo is dead and tells them about her multiple identities.
- When Sayo goes to the guesthouse to tell the cousins that dinner is ready, she stops in the rose garden to give Maria the letter and the umbrella. As in all the following games, the letter is meant to alert the family to the possibility of a 19th person and kick-start the magical narrative. It's not enough to kill everybody; the point is to make a story out of it.
- Rudolph, knowing something fishy is going on, warns Battler and Kyrie that he might be killed that night.
- X verifies that Battler doesn't remember his promise. Distraught, she commits suicide.
- George proposes to Shannon, who intentionally avoids answering. She pockets the ring to use later.
- Later that night, while Rudolph, Kyrie, Rosa and Krauss are still arguing, the servants call Gohda to the scene. Sayo then shoots all five of them with her [[Tips/Winchester M1984 Sawed off\|Winchester]], loaded with 4+1 bullets. Remember, only she has access to live ammunition; all the other guns have blanks.
- Nobody other than Sayo actually kills anyone in Legend, since **Genji, Kumasawa, and Nanjo are not killers**.
- Kinzo's body, which was hidden in the boiler room, is burned in the incinerator. Later in the day the stench will fill the mansion, prompting its discovery.
- With Eva and Hideyoshi's help, the servants spend the night creating the crime scene in the garden storehouse. They paint the magic circle and disfigure the bodies, then dress X as Shannon and put the ring George gave Sayo on her finger. They place the corpse where her face can't be seen clearly.
- Since Shannon is a manufactured identity, for the purposes of the red truth this counts as Shannon having died (Rule Z).
- Before morning, they splatter some blood on the doorknob of Natsuhi's room.
- Eva also leaves the receipt in the gap in Kinzo's study door before morning, to set Natsuhi up later.
- By morning, Kumasawa hasn't quite finished cleaning the blood in the parlor, as someone later notices.
- Kanon (Sayo) acts as though he's reporting the magic circle to Genji so the others will follow them and discover the crime scene. Importantly, Eva and Hideyoshi are adamant that none of the children enter the shed, especially that they don't get close to Shannon's corpse. If George were allowed to look at her, he might notice she's someone else.
- Natsuhi goes up to Kinzo's study and pretends to talk to him. We see a conversation between the two of them because of Rule Y.
- Eva tries to frame Natsuhi with the receipt trick, but it doesn't work. Thanks, Battler.
- In the kitchen, Maria tells Battler that Beatrice is right there with them. Of course, she's referring to Sayo.
- Eva and Hideyoshi feel secure in their deal with Sayo, so they retire to their room.
- Sayo kills them, then creates the appearance of a closed room by pretending, as Kanon, to find the door closed in an implausible way when they discover the bodies. She and the servants paint the magic symbol, mutilate the bodies and plant the stakes in their heads.
- ==Alternatively, in the narration, Kanon and Kumasawa go to fetch a pair of wire cutters because they see a magic circle on the door and the two inside aren't answering. In reality, they paint the circle, and Sayo uses the wire cutters to break in, kill them and plant the stakes. There are no witnesses to this scene other than the servants, and the room was never confirmed in red to be a closed room, so it isn't one.==
- With very little time left before the others arrive, Kanon can only leave a letter with "Praise my name" on it instead of a longer one.
- Sayo rushes to the boiler room, splatters a bag of fake blood on her chest, leaves the stake on the floor and pretends Kanon has been stabbed. Nanjo fakes an attempt at first aid and later declares Kanon dead. The red truth never claims that Kanon was dead, and his corpse isn't mentioned again, so Sayo could have got up and slipped away at any point.
- All the survivors hide in the study. This time, they've prepared a letter for Genji to keep in his pocket and place on the table when nobody is looking. Maria finds it, and Natsuhi correctly guesses that one of the servants might have put it there. She then kicks them out.
- Maria and the servants go to the parlor, and Sayo shows up after reloading her gun. She tells Maria to turn away and sing, and Maria complies.
- Nanjo and Kumasawa have underestimated Sayo's madness and don't expect her to turn against them. They beg for their lives, but she kills them anyway.
- Sayo lures everyone out by calling Kinzo's study, and they take the bait.
- When they reach the parlor, they see the crime scene. Natsuhi finds a letter telling her to go to the portrait room alone, and, believing she's the only armed person, she agrees.
- Natsuhi and Sayo shoot at each other in front of the portrait, but only Natsuhi dies because her rifle has blanks. We see this as the bullet being stopped by magic.
- The cousins find Sayo in front of Natsuhi's body, but it's already midnight and the bombs explode. Game over.
- By the end of the second day, **A great many humans on this island die. No one escapes, all die.** ==Serve come punto?==

In this reconstruction, the identities of all the corpses are accounted for. X's presence is explained by her temporarily assuming the role of Shannon. After all, Kanon is right there. 

It's important to note that the body in the shed, identified as "Shannon", appears in a scene where literally everyone except Kumasawa is present, whether alive or dead. We find it very difficult to argue that Kumasawa was disguising herself as Shannon there. An old lady shouldn't be able to pass easily for a young woman, even in a shadowy corner with her face hidden. This matters because it brings the number of bodies on Rokkenjima to 17. Apart from details like this, it wouldn't be difficult to argue that X isn't needed for the story to work, but the need for a 17th person is hidden in the gaps of the episodes in several places.

At the end of Legend, we discover that the bottled letter has been found by the police. After that, in Purgatory, we see the characters engaging in something like a book club discussion of the events of Legend of the Golden Witch, which is explicitly called an episode. Then Beatrice appears and shows magic to everyone. Again, Battler serves as a parallel to the process of trying to understand what happened, because according to Sayo, he's the person who should have understood it in the first place. This is Sayo's endless magic: the magical version of people debating the events of the letter for the first time.

In the ??? tea party, Bernkastel has a spat with Beato, then she gives hints to Battler and by extension to the people trying to solve the events. This is framed to look like she is talking to the reader, after all, the players of the visual novel are trying to understand what happened just as much as the people in the future are.

## Turn of the Golden Witch

This is our original theory for Turn of the Golden Witch.

### [[Long Form Theories/Theory for Turn of the Golden witch\|Theory for Turn of the Golden witch]]

_~ published on 28/08/2025_

This one is an improvement on Legend, but it's still fairly contrived in places. There are also a few important differences between it and our current outline, both in what we left out and in what we've changed our minds about. We'll address those here.

When the bottled letter of Legend of the Golden Witch was found, all the people looking for clues to solve the mystery of Rokkenjima were instead given a magical explanation, and that shaped the conversation around it. Later, the second bottle was retrieved, sparking a new round of discussion in which, unlike the first time, the public was already primed to expect magic.

As a result, we're shown a whole prologue in which Beato, in the tea party, backs up her argument against Bernkastel and us readers. This isn't part of the game described in the letter; it takes place in the magical framework surrounding the discussions.

As evidence, while talking to Shannon, Beato even references Bern's private conversation with Battler in Legend's ??? tea party. It's a cheeky jab showing that she was listening.

It should be noted that whenever we see magic on screen, everyone involved is either complicit or dead by the end of the scene. The bottled letter might describe Beato killing Jessica and Kanon, "Kanon" killing Nanjo and Kumasawa, or Shannon, George and Gohda fleeing the chapel (a CLOSED chapel, mind you!). Another theme that first emerges in this episode is the magical narrative's tendency to absolve the humans, all more or less culpable in their own ways, by placing all the blame on the witch. Rosa gets a heroic scene at the end, where she cares deeply about Maria and runs away with her. Shannon and Kanon are both given heroic sacrifices. Beatrice is the only one treated as guilty.

## Banquet of the Golden Witch

After the retrieval of two bottled letters, the legend of the witch has crystallised in the minds of the witch hunters. There's only one problem: as far as we know, only two bottled letters were found. We'll set that aside. After all, Battler and Beato are playing a third game. Maybe another letter was found but never made public, maybe we as an audience aren't told about it, or maybe speculation about the events of Rokkenjima has become a self-sustaining process, allowing the tea party to continue on its own.

This is our original theory, and it basically still works.

### [[Long Form Theories/Theory for Banquet of the Golden Witch\|Theory for Banquet of the Golden witch]]

_~ published on 21/01/2026_

There's enough wiggle room in Banquet for multiple working solutions, but in our opinion, this reconstruction works best.

- Beatrice meets Maria in the rose garden and gives her the usual letter.
- Like in Legend, Eva and Hideyoshi are Sayo's two accomplices this time.
- On the first night, all the conspirators except Nanjo are on their way to kill everyone in the parlor, but for some reason a fight breaks out among them. All of them are killed except Sayo.
